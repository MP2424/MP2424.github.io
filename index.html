03 —

Third-Party Risk × AI Governance

Third-party risk is the backbone of my procurement work. Before any ICT vendor is onboarded, I lead the risk screening: reviewing NDA, DPA and Technical & Organisational Measures (TOMs), checking ISO 27001 supplier clauses and change-of-control provisions, and conducting due diligence against DORA compliance and nDSG/GDPR data-protection obligations. In the post-merger integration between Helvetia and Baloise, this meant re-screening a large ICT vendor portfolio against a harmonised risk framework as contracts were consolidated.

AI governance is the natural extension of that work. As AI vendors and AI-enabled tools enter the same third-party stack, the same questions apply — data lineage, sub-processor exposure, explainability, model change control, lock-in — just with new failure modes. What sets my angle apart is that I don't only assess AI vendors from the buyer side: I design, build and deploy AI systems myself, mostly as local, privacy-preserving LLM pipelines specifically because of nDSG and DORA compliance constraints. That means I evaluate AI vendor risk with a practitioner's understanding of where the risk actually lives in the stack — not just what a vendor's questionnaire answers claim.

I'm actively targeting roles at the intersection of Third-Party Risk Management and AI Governance in Swiss regulated financial services, where procurement risk discipline and hands-on AI fluency are both needed and rarely found in the same person.
